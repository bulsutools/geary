Failing one class at BulSU triggers a warning, while multiple failures in one term quickly trigger prerequisite locks, reduced unit loads, academic probation, or even disqualification. Here is the exact seven-step delinquency ladder printed in the Student Manual.

## BulSU's Progressive Academic Delinquency Tiers

Every semester, your College Dean and unit head review failing grades (`5.00`) and drops under these minimum rules:

| Failures / Drops in One Term | Resulting Standing | What Happens Next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 failing grade** | Warning | You receive an official warning from your College Dean. |
| **2 dropped or failed subjects** | Prerequisite block | You cannot enroll in next-term requisite subjects. With Dean approval, you may take minor subjects ahead without exceeding your curriculum unit cap. |
| **3 failed subjects (below 50% of load)** | Academic Probation | You are placed on probation next semester and your unit head cuts your academic load proportionally. |
| **On probation + 2 failures or drops** | Dropped from course | You are removed from your current program, though you may apply to shift to another program if the receiving Dean accepts and the VPAA approves. |
| **50% to 75% failed subjects** | Suggested shift | You are advised to shift to another program, subject to acceptance by the receiving Dean and approval by the VPAA. |
| **More than 75% failed subjects** | Disqualified | You are disqualified from continuing your studies at BulSU. |
| **On probation + 50% or more failed subjects** | Disqualified | You are disqualified from continuing your studies at BulSU. |

Notice two details students often miss:

1. Dropping subjects counts alongside failing grades in the **2-subject prerequisite rule** and the **probation 2-subject removal rule**, so withdraw or drop carefully.
2. Going through warning, probation, or shifting **does not extend** your maximum course completion deadline (4 years for 2-year courses, 6 years for 4-year courses, 7.5 years for 5-year courses).

## Can You Graduate With an Irregular Record or Past Failure?

Having an irregular schedule or failing a class earlier in college does not block graduation by itself, as long as you **pass all required curriculum subjects**, resolve every `Inc.` or `4.00`, hold zero active disciplinary holds, settle all financial accountabilities, and complete at least one year of residency before graduation.

Also note how failures affect honors and financial aid:

- **Latin Honors** require no grade lower than `2.0` in any subject across your entire program.
- **President's List and Dean's List** require no grade lower than `2.0` in your last two consecutive semesters.
- **Student Assistantships and most scholarships** require zero failing or incomplete grades in the preceding semester.

## Support When You Fall Behind

If you end up on academic probation or struggle with multiple subjects, two manual-backed steps help you recover:

- **Guidance Center Follow-Up:** The Guidance and Counseling Services Center conducts individual follow-up sessions specifically for probationary students and students with low grades to identify root causes and rebuild study coping skills. See [BulSU Guidance and Counseling Services Guide](/articles/bulsu-guidance-and-counseling-guide).
- **Special Classes:** If you need to repeat an unoffered subject as an irregular student, you can join or petition for a special class with at least 10 requesting students, subject to your Dean's approval. Details are in [Academic Load, Prerequisites, and Residency](/articles/academic-load-prerequisites-and-residency).

If you do face dismissal, BulSU still issues your transfer credentials once your clearance is signed. Read [How to Obtain Transfer Credentials](/articles/how-to-obtain-transfer-credentials).
