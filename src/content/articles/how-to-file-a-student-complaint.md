If a classmate, faculty member, non-teaching staff, or university administrator treats you unfairly, abuses authority, or provides poor instruction or service, BulSU gives you a formal path called **Student Grievance Intervention** under Part III, Section H of the manual.

## Who Can File a Grievance?

One strict limit applies: **you must be the direct victim** of the unfair treatment. Friends, classmates, or parents cannot file a formal grievance on your behalf.

Complaints are received and facilitated by the **Director for Student Welfare (DSW)**. The DSW screens validity, tries mediation when possible, and pushes offices for action without acting as judge or disciplinary officer.

**What if your complaint is against the DSW?** Report it directly to the **Dean of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS)**, who handles the case in the DSW's place.

## Step 1: Try Informal Resolution First

Before filing formal paperwork, the manual strongly encourages (though does not force) you to talk directly with the person involved as soon as the issue happens. You may also send them a **written letter** describing the concern, and they must reply promptly through a dialogue or written response.

## Step 2: Formal Complaint Processing and Escalation

For **grade consultation and transparency grievances** (such as an instructor refusing to explain grades or posting grades without consultation), Student Government's **TAMBAD / SIWALAT** mechanism provides an immediate academic ladder: prepare a narrative report with your personal details, course information, and a clear account of what happened, submit it to your **Class Mayor**, and escalate through your **Local Student Council** to college administration or OSAS (and via official SG Google Forms when active).

For broader misconduct, abuse of authority, poor service, or unfair treatment, escalate formally through either the **Student Government** office or directly to the **Director for Student Welfare (DSW)**:

1. **Validity screening:** The DSW reviews your written complaint and supporting details.
2. **Endorsement to the concerned office:** If valid, the DSW sends formal representations to the college, department, or office involved for action.
3. **Active follow-ups:** The DSW tracks and follows up until the office resolves the complaint.
4. **Automatic escalation for inaction:** If the office ignores or stalls the endorsement, the DSW elevates the case to the next higher office along with an added complaint of inaction, continuing up the chain until it reaches the **University President**.

## Filing Disciplinary Complaints or Holding a Student Assembly

- **If seeking disciplinary penalties (including harassment, bullying, or abuse under RA 7877 / RA 10627):** File sworn written statements, witness affidavits, and documentary evidence directly with your college or campus **Student Discipline Committee** as covered in [Understanding University Disciplinary Procedures](/articles/understanding-university-disciplinary-procedures). For gender-based sexual harassment concerns (covered under the Anti-Sexual Harassment Act **RA 7877**, Safe Spaces Act **RA 11313**, and Committee on Decorum and Investigation **CODI** procedures), contact the **BulSU Gender and Development (GAD) Center** (`@gadcbulsu`, `gadc.eso.rde@bulsu.edu.ph`), which conducts Legal Aid Clinics and posts guidance on BulSU's *Enhanced Guidelines on Student Conduct, Discipline, and Grievance Intervention* and harassment reporting flowcharts.
- **If groups seek peaceful assemblies to redress grievances:** Submit a formal position paper for dialogue first. To hold a peaceful on-campus assembly, recognized sponsors must file a written request with the **University President at least two (2) school days before** the event or announcement, listing place, duration, estimated participants, course/year/section, invited speakers, and signatures of the org president and adviser, while keeping gates, roads, and regular classes clear.
