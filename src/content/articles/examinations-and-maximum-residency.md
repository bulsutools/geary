Midterm and final examinations follow the official university schedule, and completion timelines set a hard limit on how many academic years a subsidized student may remain enrolled in a program.

## Examination rules and exemptions

To sit for scheduled midterm or final examinations, you must present an **examination permit** signed by the administering instructor or professor. Students are also expected to settle financial obligations before enrolling in the following term.

Two special exam rules apply depending on your academic standing:

- **Final exam exemption:** You may be exempted from taking the final examination if your pre-final grade is at least **`1.5`** and you have completed all course requirements.
- **Special and removal examinations:** Taking an exam outside the regular schedule or sitting for a removal exam requires the subject teacher's recommendation and formal approval from your academic unit head.

## Maximum residency limits

Undergraduate programs cap the maximum number of years you may stay enrolled to finish your curriculum:

| Program Length | Maximum Residency Period |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2-year course | **4 years** |
| 4-year course | **6 years** |
| 5-year course | **7.5 years** |

Official **Leave of Absence (LOA)** terms do not count against your residency clock ([LOA rules](/articles/dropping-subjects-and-loa)). Working students may apply for residency exemption through their College Dean's recommendation and approval by the Vice President for Academic Affairs (`VPAA`).

Students who exceed maximum residency may continue studying **only after forfeiting government tuition subsidies**, which requires paying the tuition fees prescribed by the Finance Department.
